# About Page

## About Wildnature
Wildnature is a Community Interest Company based in the UK. We run nature-based camps and mentoring for boys aged 13–17, led by experienced guides who know how to work with young people.
We've been doing this work for years. It began simply: a belief that adolescent boys need real challenge, honest company, and time away from screens and schedules. That belief hasn't changed.



## What we believe
Teens need to do hard things.
Not dangerous things — hard things. Things that require focus, patience, and grit. When a boy makes fire from scratch for the first time, or spends a night alone and makes it through, something happens that a certificate can't capture.
Good guides matter.
The men around the fire matter as much as the fire itself. Our mentors are not instructors delivering a curriculum. They're people who've done their own work, who listen, and who know the difference between stepping in and stepping back.
Nature is the right environment.
Woodland strips away the noise. It demands attention. It doesn't care about status or social media — and that's exactly the point. What happens in five days in the forest can mark a boy in ways that last for years.



## How we work
Our camps follow a deliberate structure: arrival, community-building, skills, challenge, reflection, return. Each stage is intentional. Each has a purpose. Nothing is accidental.
Participation in every activity is voluntary. No boy is forced to share, perform, or prove anything. What matters is that he shows up, pays attention, and gives it a go.
Every programme is fully risk-assessed, run in compliance with the UK’s safeguarding law, and staffed by Enhanced DBS-checked mentors. Safety is not a box we tick. It's the ground everything else is built on.



## Our structure
Wildnature Rites of Passage CIC is a Community Interest Company — a legal structure for organisations that exist for community benefit, not private profit. We carry an asset lock: any surplus is reinvested in the work. Please see more on CICs here. Our governance is held by three Founding Directors, each accountable for the integrity and ethos of the organisation.

# What we do Page
Fire. Shelter. Knife. A night alone. Five days that count.

Our flagship camp is a five-day residential in UK woodland for boys aged 13–17. It's practical, structured, and demanding — in the best sense. Boys come in as strangers and leave having made fire, built shelter, and spent a night alone under trees they felled and shaped themselves.



## The five-day camp
### Arrival
Phones are set aside at the start. Boys cross into a different kind of time — no notifications, no metrics, no audience. The first day is about learning who else is here, how the place works, and what the week will ask of them.

### Skills and community
Fire-making. Shelter-building. Knife craft and wood carving. Tracking. Cooking from scratch. These are not activities on a grid — they're skills that take focus and reward patience. Boys work alongside one another and mentors who know what they're doing. Trust builds fast when the work is real.

### Circle
Morning and evening, the group gathers. Boys check in, share what's present, and listen to each other and to themselves. It's not group therapy. It's what men have done around fires for as long as there have been fires.

### The Quest
At the midpoint of camp, each boy spends a night alone in the shelter he's built. He's within clear boundaries. Mentors are nearby. Welfare checks happen through the night.
It's not a survival test. It's a night alone with no noise. What he finds there is his own business.

### Return
He comes back the next morning. The group is there to meet him. What he carried out there — and what he brings back — is named and acknowledged. Then breakfast.


### What the evidence says
An independent evaluation carried out in 2016 found significant and lasting positive change in the boys who attended Wildnature's camp. Greater confidence. Stronger peer relationships. A growing sense of identity and capability.
Parents described sons who came home more grounded. Mentors with backgrounds in youth work and counselling described the camp as among the most effective environments for young people they had encountered.


### Who the camps are for
Young people aged 13–17. Including those who find school difficult. Those who lack positive male role models at home. Those whose parents sense that something is missing and don't quite know what.
Concessionary places are available.

Safeguarding & Duty of Care
Every boy who comes to a Wildnature camp is entrusted to us by his family. That trust is the most important thing we hold.
Our camps are genuinely challenging. That's part of the point. But every moment of difficulty is held inside a careful, experienced framework — and the welfare of each young person is never one consideration among many. It is the first one.



Our approach
Wildnature is committed to providing a safe, respectful environment for all participants aged 13–17. Our safeguarding approach is built on three principles:
Every boy has the right to be protected from harm.
Safeguarding is everyone's responsibility — not just the designated lead's.
Good safeguarding begins long before camp starts, in how we recruit, train and hold ourselves accountable.



The people responsible
Our Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL) holds overall responsibility for implementing our Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy, managing concerns, and maintaining relationships with local safeguarding partners and statutory agencies.
A Deputy DSL supports this role and is available whenever the DSL cannot be reached. Contact details for both are shared with all staff and volunteers before every programme, and are available to parents and carers on request.
Safeguarding responsibilities are embedded in our governance. Our Directors hold legal and ethical accountability. No significant change to the safeguarding policy can be made without their approval.



Recruitment and training
All staff and volunteers undergo Enhanced DBS checks before working with young people. References are verified, identity confirmed, and safeguarding is a standard part of every interview.
Once in role, all team members receive annual safeguarding training, a Code of Conduct briefing, and ongoing supervision. No one works unsupervised with young people. Agreed staff-to-participant ratios are maintained throughout every programme.



Risk and challenge
Our camps include activities that are intentionally stretching — fire-making, shelter-building, the solo overnight. These are not risks to be removed. They are the point.
Each activity is subject to a thorough risk assessment. Dynamic risk assessment continues throughout every camp. At least one qualified first aider is present at all times. Emergency procedures are in place and rehearsed.
Challenge at Wildnature is always held within consent, care, and clear safeguarding oversight.



Raising a concern
If something doesn't feel right — before, during, or after a programme — we want to hear from you.
Concerns can be raised directly with our Designated Safeguarding Lead, through our Complaints Officer, or via our Whistleblowing process. If a concern relates to a young person at immediate risk, we will always refer to statutory agencies without delay.
External routes are always available, including the NSPCC Helpline (0808 800 5000) and local authority children's services. We will never discourage anyone from using them.
Full details of our Safeguarding, Complaints, and Whistleblowing policies are available on request.

PAGE 5 — GET INVOLVED
Get Involved
This work happens because people show up for it. If you want to be one of them, here's how.



Volunteer as a mentor
Our volunteer mentors are the core of every camp. You don't need to be a wilderness expert or a qualified therapist. You need to be honest, good with young people, and willing to be present for a week in the woods.
What that looks like in practice:
Attending pre-camp training and safeguarding briefings
Showing up as yourself — not performing, not fixing, not lecturing
Working alongside experienced programme leaders
Being available to young people across the full five days of camp
In return, you'll receive structured preparation, clear expectations, experienced supervision, and the kind of week that's hard to explain to people who weren't there.
We're looking for people who are grounded, dependable, and genuinely interested in young people. If that's you, get in touch.



Work with us
We're always interested in people who combine practical skill with genuine presence — outdoor education, first aid, facilitation, youth work.
Everyone who works with Wildnature is inducted into our approach, policies, and values before working with young people. Equality and inclusion are built into how we operate. We take safeguarding seriously, and we expect the same of everyone we work with.
If you're looking for work that's demanding and genuinely meaningful, we'd like to hear from you.



Partner with us
We work with schools, community organisations, landowners, and other charities that care about young people and the natural world.
If your organisation works with boys aged 13–17, supports outdoor or experiential learning, or holds land that could serve this work, let's talk.
We approach every partnership as a real collaboration, not a referral arrangement.



Training
Everyone who works or volunteers with Wildnature receives training in safeguarding, health and safety, and risk management, plus a thorough induction into our approach.
This is ongoing. We run regular team learning sessions, reflective practice, and annual reviews. We think the best guides are always still learning — and we build that expectation into the culture from the start.

Fund and Support Wildnature
Wildnature exists to make a particular experience available to boys who need it: five days in woodland, real skills, honest company, and a night alone that belongs entirely to them.
None of that happens without investment — in trained mentors, safeguarding infrastructure, good equipment, and the ongoing care that makes the work possible season after season.



What the evidence shows
An independent evaluation of Wildnature's Boys Rites of Passage Camp (University of Brighton, 2016) found significant and lasting positive change across three areas:
A renewed connection with the natural world
Deeper peer relationships and a stronger sense of belonging
Growing confidence through practical challenge and real skill
Parents described sons who came home more mature, more grounded, and more open. Mentors with backgrounds in counselling described it as among the most powerful environments for young people they had encountered.

Every pound we receive goes toward the quality, safety, and reach of the work. We do not exist for private profit.
